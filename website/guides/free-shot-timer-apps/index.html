Best shot timer apps compared: free and paid

Updated 26 November 2026 · 8 min read

I make one of these apps, Simple Shot Timer, so this page doesn't crown a winner. It sticks to what the store listings say: whether an app hears your shots, which phones it runs on, what it costs, whether Google Play labels it as containing ads, and what the stores' privacy labels say.

The short answer

Which shot timer app is best depends on how you train. For dry fire alone, a par timer that only beeps does the job; ShootOnTime is a popular free one on iPhone. If you want your splits, you need an app that hears your shots. Three of those cost $2.99 up front and have few ratings so far. Of the rest, half are free downloads that sell an upgrade or a subscription, and half are free with nothing to buy. Simple Shot Timer is free with nothing to buy, hears your shots on Android and iPhone, and collects no data.

Free apps

Simple Shot Timer

My app, so read this one with that in mind. Free with no ads, no purchases and no account, and open source under GPLv3. It hears your shots in every mode, has a Par mode with repeat cycles and a rest, times stages of up to 200 seconds and keeps up to 5,000 strings with CSV export. Both stores list no data collected. It has 16 ratings at 4.4 on Google Play and none yet on the US App Store, and like any phone timer it is limited by the phone's microphone and speaker. Google Play · App Store

ShootOnTime: Par Timer

A par timer for iPhone with about 1,200 ratings at 4.9, free with no in-app purchases, and no data collected according to its privacy label. Its listing says plainly that it doesn't detect shots, so it suits par work and dry fire. App Store

Better Shot Timer

A free iPhone shot timer with no in-app purchases and no data collected according to its privacy label. Its listing says it logs every shot through audio detection, for live and dry fire. It has 76 ratings at 4.6 and hasn't been updated since January 2024. App Store

Standby: IPSC Shot Timer

Free on iPhone, Apple Watch and Android, with no in-app purchases. Google Play lists it as Standby: IPSC Shot Timer and the App Store as IPSC Shot Timer; it is a different app from the Android one below. Its listing says it counts every shot with the phone's microphone, and that it has no ads, no tracking and no account. Both stores list no data collected. It has 12 ratings at 4.7 on the App Store and was updated on 24 November 2026. Google Play · App Store

IPSC Shot Timer

A free Android shot timer by Stoliarov Ivan with over 100,000 downloads and about 4,000 ratings at 3.0. Its listing calls it a shot-activated timer for live or dry fire, depending on sensitivity, and describes it as a beta version. It was last updated in May 2025. Google Play labels it as containing ads, and its Data safety section says no data is collected. Google Play

Free with purchases

Shot Timer – Measure & Improve

A shot timer for Android and iPhone that detects shots as spikes in volume. It is free with ads, and an in-app purchase removes them: $2.99 on the App Store, $1.99 on Google Play. Its App Store label lists identifiers not linked to you, and Google Play says it may collect app activity and shares no data. It has 95 ratings at 1.9 on Google Play and none yet on the App Store. Google Play · App Store

Shot Timer – Range Trainer

An iPhone shot timer with a microphone sensitivity setting, free with a Premium in-app purchase for $6.99 and no data collected according to its label. Its listing names the sensitivity setting but doesn't say outright that it detects shots. It has 458 ratings at 4.1 and hasn't been updated since February 2024. App Store

Par Timer Pro

A par timer. Its Google Play listing and its website say it doesn't detect gunshots. It is free to try, with in-app purchases of $9.99 to $29.99 on Google Play, which says the app may collect app activity, crash logs and device IDs and shares no data. It has 20 ratings at 3.3 there. On 26 November 2026 its App Store page returned “not found”, although the maker's website still links to it, so the iPhone version couldn't be checked. Google Play

PlinkPal Shot Timer & Dry Fire

A shot timer and dry-fire app for iPhone, Android and watches, which hears shots through the microphone. It is free to download with Premium purchases from $3.99 to $59.99, and its terms describe a paid subscription; which features are free isn't clear from the listing. Its App Store label lists data linked to you, and Google Play says it shares personal info and device IDs with third parties. It has 22 ratings at 3.9 on the App Store and 47 at 2.9 on Google Play. Google Play · App Store

Range Buddy – Shooting Drills

An all-in-one training app: it times shots through the phone's microphone or a Bluetooth timer, with a drill library and video scoring. Free to download with a Pro subscription, $7.99 a month or $49.99 a year on the App Store. Its App Store label lists identifiers used to track you and data linked to you, such as location and contact info, and Google Play says it shares personal and financial info. It has 19 ratings at 4.8 on the App Store and 48 at 4.2 on Google Play. Google Play · App Store

Paid apps

Four of the fourteen apps here charge up front. None has more than 63 ratings on either store.

Airsoft Shot Timer

Made for airsoft: its listing says it detects airsoft gunshots, even in noisy environments. It costs $2.99 on the App Store; on Google Play it is free to install with a $2.99 in-app purchase. The App Store label lists usage data not linked to you, and Google Play says it shares device IDs. It has 35 ratings at 3.4 on the App Store and 63 at 2.5 on Google Play. Google Play · App Store

ShotTimer Pro – Shooting Timer

An iPhone shot timer for $2.99 with no in-app purchases. Its listing says it detects shots with the microphone and records split times, and its privacy label lists no data collected. Version 1.0 came out in April 2026, and it has one rating so far. App Store

ShotTimer: Splits & Par Time

An iPhone shot timer for $2.99 with no in-app purchases; it needs iOS 26. Its listing says the iPhone's microphone picks up your shots, and that dry-fire clicks are usually too quiet to detect. The privacy label lists no data collected. Version 1.0 came out in October 2026, and it has no ratings yet. App Store

Dry Fire Timer & Par Drills

A par timer for iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch, $8.99 on the US App Store. Its listing calls that a one-time purchase with no subscription, no ads and no account; the maker's website gives $19.99 as the price after a trial period. The listing doesn't mention shot detection, and the website says the app doesn't listen for gunshots. The privacy label lists no data collected. It has 9 ratings at 4.6 and was updated in November 2026. App Store

Which one is best for you

  • Dry fire only: any par timer will do, whether it hears shots or not. Par timer vs. shot timer explains the difference.
  • Live fire: pick an app that hears your shots, and test it on your range before you rely on it. Fire a string with a known number of shots and check that the count matches.
  • Free or freemium: check what the free version includes before you build a routine around an app.
  • Paying: a paid app still works with your phone's microphone and speaker. If you want a louder beep and hardware made for the range, that is what a dedicated timer is for; shot timer app or dedicated shot timer? compares the two.
  • Privacy: read the store's privacy label. A timer doesn't need an account or your location to work.

Whichever app you pick, how to use your phone as a shot timer covers the setup that makes the times hold up.

How this was checked

I read each app's US listing on the App Store and Google Play, and the developer's website where there is one, on 26 November 2026. I haven't tested every app on a range, so “hears your shots” is what each listing says. Apps that only work with separate hardware, such as a Bluetooth timer or laser targets, are left out. Apps change, and if something here is out of date or wrong, open an issue on GitHub and I'll fix it.
